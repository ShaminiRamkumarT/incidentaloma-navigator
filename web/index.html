How it works

A trained reader, then rules you can audit

Most incidental findings are harmless, but each organ has its own guideline with its own size cut-offs and risk rules. The decision part is deliberately not a black box.

01 · Read

Pull out the lesions

A model trained on 600 synthetic reports labels each word: organ, size, density, features, and what the radiologist recommended.

02 · Apply

Run the guideline

A rules engine applies Bosniak, Fleischner, ACR, Bone-RADS and the others. Every verdict points to a threshold and a paper.

03 · Flag

Compare with the report

Under-calls (too little follow-up) and over-calls (too much) are ranked by severity, with missed specialist referrals at the top.

Flags. Under-call: the guideline asks for something the report leaves out (high severity when a specialist referral is missing). Over-call: the report asks for follow-up the guideline does not. Any imaging meets an imaging requirement, and a referral covers one.

Simplified to the type of action (no follow-up, imaging, referral, hormonal work-up), not the exact interval. When a report does not state the patient's risk or age, any answer the guideline allows for some patient is accepted.
